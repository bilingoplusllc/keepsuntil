What the
freezer buys

The other multiplier in the data, and the one that goes the right way. No bars here: the question is how many times, not how long. The figure is set larger the larger it is.

  • Eggs, raw whites and yolks Fridge 2 to 4 days → freezer 12 months 90×
  • Quiche Fridge after opening 3 to 5 days → freezer 2 to 3 months 18×
  • Anchovies, canned Fridge after opening 3 to 4 days → freezer 2 months 15×
  • Butter Fridge from purchase 1 to 2 months → freezer 6 to 9 months 4.5×
  • Bacon Fridge from purchase 1 week → freezer 1 month 4.3×
  • Bacon, uncured Fridge 4 to 7 days → freezer 3 to 4 weeks 4×

Three foods in this sample carry the opposite verdict. Eggs in the shell, hard-boiled eggs and lactose-free milk are all marked freezing not recommended — the freezer is not an option there, at any multiple.

Where the figures come from. Every span on this page is the USDA FoodKeeper's own wording for that food and that place, reproduced without adjustment. FoodKeeper data is published by the U.S. Department of Agriculture and is in the public domain.

What is worked out here. The multipliers are not in the source. Each one divides the longer figure by the longer figure of the shorter state, so it is the mildest version of the gap, not the most alarming: 1,825 ÷ 4 = 456.25, printed as 456×. Multipliers are rounded to whole numbers above 10 and to one decimal below. A span is a quality figure, not a moment of failure — food that looks, smells or sounds wrong goes out whatever the date says.
