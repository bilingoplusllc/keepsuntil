Read this once

A scale that misleads is worse than a table.

All three rulers are linear. Equal distance is equal time. Nothing is squeezed and there is no logarithmic scale here — on a log scale the five years would look about four times the four days instead of four hundred and fifty-six, which is a lie of two orders of magnitude.

Ruler 2 is a window cut out of ruler 1; ruler 3 is a window cut out of ruler 2. Each is redrawn at full length with its magnification printed between them — ×30.4, then ×8.6, so ruler 3 is ×260.7 the scale of ruler 1. The three are never the same scale and never pretend to be.

Opening the can costs a factor of 456. 1825 days ÷ 4 days. Take the shorter end of the 3-to-4-day figure and it is 608. We print the gentler of the two.

Dates are counted in days, not in calendar years. The cupboard answer is your date plus 1825 days, the source’s own number. A leap year in the way makes that land a day before the calendar five-year anniversary, and we would rather be a day short than a day long.

Source

USDA FoodKeeper lists canned chicken as keeping 5 years in the pantry from purchase, and 3 to 4 days refrigerated after opening. Both figures are quoted as published; the rulers, the ×456 and the dates are ours, worked out from those two numbers and nothing else.

They describe a can kept at ordinary cupboard temperature and, once opened, covered and refrigerated at 40 °F or below. They are not a verdict on the can in your hand: one that is bulging, leaking, rusted through or smells wrong is wrong whatever this page says.
