Read this once

A scale that misleads is worse than a table, so here is exactly what these rulers do.

Every ruler is linear. Equal distance is equal time, counted from zero. Nothing is squeezed, nothing is bent, and there is no logarithmic scale anywhere on this site. If a mark looks small here, it is small.

When a figure is too short to see, we cut a window. The window is shaded on the ruler it came from, redrawn full length as the next ruler down, and the magnification is printed between them — ×20.3 on this page. The two rulers are not the same scale and never pretend to be.

FoodKeeper counts a month as 30 days and a year as 365. We keep the source’s arithmetic rather than our own, so a “12 months” figure lands at 360 days — five days short of the 1-year tick above. That gap belongs to the data, and we would rather show it than tidy it away.

Not in the source

FoodKeeper publishes the figures. These three come from putting them on one ruler.

×90

What the freezer buys. Raw egg whites and yolks: 2 to 4 days in the fridge, 12 months frozen. 360 ÷ 4.

×456

What opening it costs. Canned chicken: 5 years sealed in the cupboard, 3 to 4 days once opened. 1825 ÷ 4 — and ×608 at the shorter end. See it drawn.

a date

Not a duration. Tell a food’s page the day you stored it and the ruler answers with the day it runs out, and shows where today stands on the way there.

Source

Figures: USDA FoodKeeper, public data, quoted as published. They describe quality and safe home storage for an item kept at the right temperature. They are not a verdict on the jar in your hand: a container that is bulging, leaking or smells wrong is wrong whatever any ruler says.
